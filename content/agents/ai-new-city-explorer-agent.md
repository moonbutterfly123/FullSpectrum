## How We Do It Today

You moved to a new city, or you're visiting one, and you're doing the same three things everyone does: the tourist spots, the chain coffee, the restaurant next to the hotel. You're in a new place and experiencing none of it.

The honest truth: exploring a city well is easy to want and hard to do. You're juggling what to see, where to eat, what's worth it, what's local, what's open, and the slow drift that turns 'I'll explore' into 'I'll just go to the place I know.' Multiply by a place you don't know, and you've got an adventure that stays on the surface.

So you muddle through. You hit the top-ten list, miss the neighborhoods, and leave feeling you saw the city but didn't know it. You mean to go local, and you don't.

It works, the way the tourist spots work, until you're home wishing you'd gone deeper. This is exactly the kind of preference-sensitive, location-aware, discovery-driven coordination that an agent is built to share — not to take over your trip, but to carry the finding so the city actually opens up.

## Meet Your Agent Partner

Enter your new explorer sidekick: the AI New-City Explorer Agent. Think of it as a friend who actually lives there, who knows your taste and your pace, who can find the local spot and build the day, and who never lets you eat at the chain when the gem's around the corner.

You talk to it the way you'd talk to that friend — 'I'm in Lisbon for three days, I like food and walking, I hate tourist traps, and I want to feel like I know the place' — and it gets to work. It finds the neighborhoods, it picks the spots, it builds the day, and it keeps it local.

Its job isn't to script your trip. It's to clear away the where-do-I-go paralysis so the city opens up. You stay the one deciding what's your vibe; the agent handles the finding, the routing, and the 'hey, this neighborhood's your taste, here's the day' suggestion.

And it remembers. Next city it knows your taste, your pace, and what you loved. A partner that learns your exploring, instead of forgetting it every time you fly home.

## Smart Synthesis: Putting the Pieces Together

The clever part of exploring isn't seeing a sight — it's fitting the right day to your taste. The neighborhoods that fit you. The local spots, not the chains. The pace that works. The routing that flows. And the discovery so you go deeper, not just the top-ten.

This is where the agent shines. It holds dozens of details at once — your taste, the city, the neighborhoods, the spots, the hours — and builds a day that keeps all of them honest. It's what a thoughtful local friend does, except across the whole city in the time it takes to refill your coffee.

It also balances discovery against practicality: the local gem, but open when you're there, and protecting your time so you enjoy, not march. Good synthesis is invisible — you just notice you're loving it.

And it earns your trust by showing its work: why this spot, what's local about it, what trade-off it made. So you can nudge it — more food, less walking — instead of shrugging at a mystery list. You'd get the taste-fit spots because it knows you, the local picks because it knows the city, and a 'here's your day' instead of a top-ten.

## Agents in Practice

You don't have to imagine this — versions of it are already out there. Atlas Obscura and Google Maps' AI surface local spots. Tripadvisor and Lonely Planet's AI suggest itineraries. ChatGPT and Gemini, with web search, will build a local day from one sentence.

The platforms have joined in: Yelp and Foursquare's AI finds your taste-fit spots, while Instagram and TikTok surface trending places. Citymapper and Transit route your day. And a wave of dedicated AI explorer tools — like Visit a City and trip.skift — promise a local day from a few inputs.

These tools differ in a few honest ways. Some find spots, some build days, some route. The platform-backed ones have the reviews but nudge you to the popular; the chat assistants plan widely but can't see your live location. Atlas Obscura feels like a hidden-gem finder; Yelp feels like a review site. Most are free, with the serious features behind a paywall.

So how do you pick? Want hidden spots — Atlas Obscura. Want your taste — Yelp or Foursquare. Want a day planned — ChatGPT. By reach, Google Maps and the chat tools lead, then the review sites, with the dedicated explorer tools the fast-growing newcomers.

## Who's Using It

You might wonder whether anyone's actually trusting an AI with their exploring. The short answer: billions, and you do too.

The platforms lead by sheer reach. Google Maps, Yelp, and Tripadvisor together reach billions of users exploring cities, with their AI now surfacing spots for nearly everyone. Foursquare and Instagram reach hundreds of millions.

The dedicated tools punch above their size. Atlas Obscura, Visit a City, and similar serve millions of explorers going deeper. The AI-explorer tools reach loyal users in the hundreds of thousands.

The chat assistants are the wildcard. ChatGPT and Gemini don't report 'explorer users,' but 'what should I do in X city' is one of the most common asks — a number measured in the hundreds of millions of conversations that nobody counts as a product.

The honest takeaway: AI exploring help has crossed from novelty into something nearly everyone uses, mostly through the platforms and chat tools, with the dedicated tools growing fastest. If you're considering it, you're in very large — and increasingly ordinary — company.

## What's Still Missing

For all their progress, today's explorer agents share gaps none has quite closed.

The big one is the true local. Agents find spots beautifully, but knowing the actually-local place — not the trending, not the reviewed, the real one — is still uneven. True, genuine-local discovery is still rare.

Memory across cities is another hole. Many start fresh each trip. Few remember your taste, your pace, or what you loved. A partner that learns your exploring over years is still missing.

Then there's the live layer. Plenty plan the day. Far fewer handle the changes — the closed spot, the rain, the reroute. Live day-management is still uneven.

The serendipity side is harder. Exploring isn't just the list; it's the wandering, the surprise. Agents optimize for the plan by the averages; the 'let's see what's down this street' is still yours.

Privacy looms. Your location data is sensitive, and sharing it with a commercial agent raises questions. Closing these gaps is where the next wave earns its keep.

## Building Your Own Agent

If you'd rather build this yourself, the pieces are surprisingly approachable.

You start with a system prompt that defines the agent's character — a local friend that gathers your taste, pace, and location, never books without your say-so, always shows the why behind a spot, and asks clarifying questions when you're vague. The prompt is where you teach the agent its manners and its taste.

Next come the data inputs. You capture the explorer's profile through a structured chat — taste, pace, what you've loved, your location — and store these as the day's rules so the agent reasons against them instead of guessing at your trip.

Then you wire in the real world: a places API (Google Maps, Yelp) for spots, a reviews API for quality, and a routing API (Citymapper) for the day. Whenever the agent needs a fact, it calls these like a local pulling a recommendation.

You give it a reasoning loop — gather, find, route, refine on your feedback, then explore. Each step hands evidence to the next, so nothing lands on a hunch.

And you keep a human in the loop. Before any booking, the agent shows the spot and the reason, you confirm, and it never books without you. Add a guardrail so it always flags closed or unsafe areas.

You don't need to build all of it at once. A useful first version just finds spots; routing comes once you trust the plan.

## The Bottom Line

The AI New-City Explorer Agent won't replace the wonder of discovering a place on your own, and it shouldn't try. What it does is something more practical and quietly wonderful: it brings your taste together with tireless, precise finding.

For the tourist-trap-stuck, that's the difference between the chain and the gem. For the surface-scratcher, it's the neighborhood that opens up. For everyone, it's the drift dialed down — spent exploring instead of defaulting.

The technology isn't magic. It depends on honest taste, your input about your pace, and the wisdom to wander sometimes. But where those conditions hold, the explorer agent is one of the most quietly life-improving agent applications there is: it takes the most surface-level, most easy-to-default chore in travel and handles it as your partner — then hands you the city.
