## How We Do It Today

Holiday planning is the thing you look forward to all year and dread the planning of. You want to go somewhere, your partner wants somewhere else, the kids have opinions, and the research turns into a spreadsheet that nobody reads and a decision that never comes.

The honest truth: planning a holiday is easy to want and exhausting to do. You're juggling where, when, the budget, the flights, the stays, the activities, the group's different wants, and the slow erosion of excitement that comes with the logistics. Multiply by a trip that costs real money, and you've got a decision that keeps stalling.

So you muddle through. You research for hours, get overwhelmed, and book the first thing that seems okay. You overpack the itinerary, underplan the logistics, and arrive stressed.

It works, the way okay works, until you're there wishing you'd planned better. This is exactly the kind of multi-preference, logistics-heavy, group-dependent coordination that an agent is built to share — not to take over your trip, but to carry the planning so the holiday actually delivers.

## Meet Your Agent Partner

Enter your new holiday sidekick: the AI Holiday Planning Agent. Think of it as a friend who actually enjoys planning, who knows your group and your budget, who can find the destination and build the itinerary, and who never lets the logistics eat the excitement.

You talk to it the way you'd talk to that friend — 'we want a week somewhere warm, two adults two kids, moderate budget, mix of beach and culture, and I'm overwhelmed by the options' — and it gets to work. It finds the destinations, it builds the itinerary, it books the pieces, and it balances the group's wants.

Its job isn't to pick your trip. It's to clear away the where-do-we-go paralysis so the holiday happens. You stay the one deciding what matters; the agent handles the finding, the planning, and the 'hey, this destination fits everyone, here's why' synthesis.

And it remembers. Next trip it knows your style, your budget, and what worked. A partner that learns your travel, instead of forgetting it every time you close the spreadsheet.

## Smart Synthesis: Putting the Pieces Together

The clever part of a holiday isn't picking a place — it's fitting the right trip to your group. The destination that fits the budget. The activities that fit the people. The pace that fits the week. The logistics that actually work. And the balance so everyone's happy.

This is where the agent shines. It holds dozens of details at once — your group, the budget, the dates, the wants, the destinations — and builds a trip that keeps all of them honest. It's what a thoughtful travel agent does, except across the whole world in the time it takes to refill your coffee.

It also balances ambition against logistics: the trip you want, not the one that falls apart, and protecting the budget so you enjoy it, not stress about it. Good synthesis is invisible — you just notice the trip flows.

And it earns your trust by showing its work: why this destination, what it costs, what trade-off it made. So you can nudge it — cheaper, more beach — instead of shrugging at a mystery plan. You'd get the group-fit destination because it knows everyone, the budget check because it's watching, and a 'here's the itinerary' instead of a spreadsheet.

## Agents in Practice

You don't have to imagine this — versions of it are already out there. Wanderlog and Tripadvisor's AI plan trips and build itineraries. Expedia and Kayak's AI suggests destinations and deals. ChatGPT and Gemini, with web search, will plan a whole holiday from one sentence.

The platforms have joined in: Google Travel and Airbnb's AI surface destinations and stays, while Booking.com's AI suggests the right trip. Viator and GetYourGuide use AI for activities. And a wave of dedicated AI trip-planners — like Mindtrip and Tripplanner AI — promise a full plan from a few inputs.

These tools differ in a few honest ways. Some plan, some find deals, some suggest activities. The platform-backed ones have the inventory but nudge you to book; the chat assistants plan widely but can't book. Wanderlog feels like a planner; Expedia feels like a deal. Most have a free tier, with the serious planning behind a paywall.

So how do you pick? Want an itinerary — Wanderlog. Want deals — Kayak. Want a full plan from one sentence — ChatGPT. By reach, the chat tools and platforms lead, then the trip-planners, with the dedicated AI-planners the fast-growing newcomers.

## Who's Using It

You might wonder whether anyone's actually trusting an AI with their holiday. The short answer: hundreds of millions, and you probably have too.

The platforms lead by sheer reach. Expedia, Kayak, Booking.com, and Airbnb together reach hundreds of millions of travelers, with their AI now suggesting destinations and deals. Tripadvisor reaches an enormous audience.

The trip-planners punch above their size. Wanderlog, TripIt, and similar serve tens of millions of travelers planning real itineraries. Mindtrip and similar reach loyal users in the hundreds of thousands.

The chat assistants are the rocket. ChatGPT and Gemini don't report 'holiday users,' but 'plan a trip to X' is one of the most common asks — a number measured in the hundreds of millions of conversations that nobody counts as a product.

The honest takeaway: AI holiday-planning has crossed from novelty into something hundreds of millions use, mostly through the platforms and chat tools, with the trip-planners growing fastest. If you're considering it, you're in very large — and increasingly ordinary — company.

## What's Still Missing

For all their progress, today's holiday agents share gaps none has quite closed.

The big one is the group synthesis. Agents plan beautifully for one, but balancing a family's different wants — the kid who wants the pool, the partner who wants culture — is still uneven. True, group-aware planning is still rare.

Memory across trips is another hole. Many start fresh each holiday. Few remember your style, your past trips, or what worked. A partner that learns your travel over years is still missing.

Then there's the live layer. Plenty plan the trip. Far fewer handle the changes — the flight delay, the closed activity, the reroute. Live trip-management is still uneven.

The presence side is harder. A holiday isn't just the plan; it's being there, not managing. Agents optimize for the itinerary by the averages; the 'I'll put the phone down' is still yours.

Privacy looms. Sharing your travel with a commercial agent raises questions. Closing these gaps is where the next wave earns its keep.

## Building Your Own Agent

If you'd rather build this yourself, the pieces are surprisingly approachable.

You start with a system prompt that defines the agent's character — a planner that gathers your group, budget, and wants, never books without your say-so, always shows the why behind a destination, and asks clarifying questions when you're vague. The prompt is where you teach the agent its manners and its balance.

Next come the data inputs. You capture the trip's profile through a structured chat — group, budget, dates, wants, style — and store these as the plan's rules so the agent reasons against them instead of guessing at your trip.

Then you wire in the real world: a flights API (Kayak, Skyscanner), a stays API (Airbnb, Booking), and an activities API (Viator). Whenever the agent needs a fact, it calls these like a travel agent pulling a brochure.

You give it a reasoning loop — gather, find, plan, refine on your feedback, then book. Each step hands evidence to the next, so nothing lands on a hunch.

And you keep a human in the loop. Before any booking, the agent shows the plan and the reason, you confirm, and only then does it book. Add a guardrail so it never books past your budget cap without asking.

You don't need to build all of it at once. A useful first version just plans; booking comes once you trust the plan.

## The Bottom Line

The AI Holiday Planning Agent won't replace the feeling of arriving somewhere you're excited about, and it shouldn't try. What it does is something more practical and quietly wonderful: it brings your trip together with tireless, precise planning.

For the overwhelmed, that's the difference between a spreadsheet and a trip. For the over-planned, it's the pace that works. For everyone, it's the logistics dialed down — spent enjoying instead of planning.

The technology isn't magic. It depends on honest wants, your input about your group, and the wisdom to enjoy it when you're there. But where those conditions hold, the holiday agent is one of the most quietly life-improving agent applications there is: it takes the most exciting, most logistics-heavy decision of the year and handles it as your partner — then hands you the holiday.
